A multi-zone deployment connects several zones to the same global control plane.
Applications keep calling service hostnames.
The mesh finds the zone where the destination runs and carries the request across the zone boundary.

## Components of a multi-zone deployment

{% include md/mesh/v3/multi-zone-components.md %}

The `orders` sidecar in `zone1` connects directly to the zone ingress in `zone2`.
The zone ingress then forwards the request to `payments`.
A zone egress in `zone1` doesn't change this path, because it carries only traffic to `MeshExternalService` destinations.

## Mesh-scoped zone proxies

Each zone runs a zone ingress for every mesh that receives traffic from other zones, and optionally a zone egress.
Other zones find the zone ingress through a `MeshZoneAddress`.
The zone ingress forwards the encrypted connection without decrypting it, so it doesn't need a workload identity, but the zone egress does.
The zone control plane adds the `kuma.io/listener-zoneingress: enabled` or `kuma.io/listener-zoneegress: enabled` label to each zone proxy, and a policy selects the zone proxies with that label in its top-level `targetRef`.
Not every policy supports zone proxies, so check the policy reference first.

For deployment, addressing, and policy details, see [Mesh-scoped zone proxies](/mesh/mesh-scoped-zone-proxies/).

## Identity and trust across zones

A cross-zone request succeeds only when three conditions are met:

1. A `MeshIdentity` gives a workload identity to the application sidecars in both zones. Without it, the zone control plane doesn't send the caller the endpoints of the other zone. See [Make ingress reachable from other zones](/mesh/mesh-scoped-zone-proxies/#make-ingress-reachable-from-other-zones).
1. Each zone trusts the certificate authority (CA) that signed the certificates of the other zone. With autogenerated CAs, each zone has its own CA and trust domain, and you distribute the trust yourself. See [Multi-zone meshes](/mesh/policies/meshidentity/#multi-zone-meshes).
1. A `MeshTrafficPermission` allows the SPIFFE ID of the caller from the other zone. See [Match caller identities](/mesh/policies/meshtrafficpermission/#match-caller-identities).

## Service hostnames across zones

Each zone control plane creates a `MeshService` for each local service and sends it to the other zones through the global control plane.
A caller reaches a service in one specific zone with a hostname that includes the zone, such as `payments.NAMESPACE.svc.zone2.mesh.local`.
For how the control plane builds these hostnames, see [HostnameGenerator](/mesh/concepts/#hostnamegenerator).

To give callers one hostname for a service that runs in several zones, create a `MeshMultiZoneService` on the global control plane.
Callers reach it with a hostname of the form `NAME.mzsvc.mesh.local`, and `MeshLoadBalancingStrategy` decides which zone receives each request.
For more information, see [MeshMultiZoneService](/mesh/meshmultizoneservice/).

A hostname doesn't grant access or establish trust.

## Observability across zones

The control planes configure telemetry, but they don't collect it.
Each proxy sends or exposes its own metrics, traces, and access logs:

* [`MeshMetric`](/mesh/policies/meshmetric/) exposes or exports metrics. Prometheus or an OpenTelemetry collector collects them from each zone.
* [`MeshTrace`](/mesh/policies/meshtrace/) makes the proxies create spans. Applications must forward the trace context between their own requests to produce one end-to-end trace.
* [`MeshAccessLog`](/mesh/policies/meshaccesslog/) sends access logs to a file, a TCP endpoint, or an OpenTelemetry collector.

To build a dashboard for every zone, send the data of all zones to the same backends, and label it with the mesh, zone, workload, and service.
